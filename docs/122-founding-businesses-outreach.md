# 122. Getting the first real businesses listed: a pack for the owner

Written 18 November 2026. OurValleys is live but its directory holds only one labelled fictional business, so it looks empty and gives search engines almost nothing to index. Nothing in this pack has been sent to anyone: contacting businesses in the owner's name is an approval gate (`AGENTS.md`), so this is preparation, ready to use.

This is not legal advice. The point of the compliance notes is to keep outreach simple and safe.

## 1. Ground rules

- **Real, willing businesses only.** Never list a business that has not agreed, and never invent or copy facts about one. A business can be added only by its owner (or someone they have authorised), or suggested through the public form, which goes to a private review and is never published automatically.
- **Say what is true.** The site is free, there are no payments, and a new listing is reviewed before it appears. Do not promise visitors, rankings or sales.
- **Know which routes need consent.** Under the UK rules on electronic marketing (PECR), an unsolicited marketing **email, text or private social-media message** counts as electronic mail. To a sole trader or partnership it needs their consent (they are treated as individuals); to a limited company's corporate address it is allowed if you say who you are and offer a way to opt out. **Live marketing calls** must be checked against the TPS and CTPS registers and any earlier objection, unless the person has agreed to be called. See the ICO's business-to-business marketing guidance.
- **Routes that are not electronic mail:** speaking to someone **in person** (calling in to a shop is ordinary conversation), a **public** post or comment that is not a private message (within the platform's and group's rules), a **noticeboard or flyer**, and **replying when someone has asked or written to you first**. Start with these. Do not buy or scrape a list, and keep a note of anyone who says no, and do not contact them again.
- **One message, one ask.** Ask them to look at the site and, if they like it, start a free listing.

## 2. Where to start

- **Places.** Start where the site already has pages: Treorchy, Porth, Aberdare, Mountain Ash, Ferndale, Pontypridd, and Tonypandy, where the fictional demonstration business sits. The reference data holds 79 places, so any other is possible too.
- **Categories.** There are 24 in the directory. The ones most worth having first, because residents search for them most: plumbing and heating, electrical, food and drink (cafes, takeaways, bakers), hair and beauty, home trades, health and wellbeing, and community organisations and clubs. A community organisation or club is often easier to reach, and its events are what make the Events page worth visiting.
- **Order.** Aim for five to ten real listings in one or two places before widening. A small place that looks well covered is better than many that look thin.

## 3. What the business does (so you can explain it in a sentence)

1. Go to ourvalleys.co.uk and choose **List your business** (in the header and on the home page). Create an account with an email address and a password, and confirm the email (verification is required).
2. On the page titled **Create your free business website**, add the business name, category and location. A starter website preview appears straight away. Then complete the rest in the business dashboard: a short description, contact details, opening hours and a picture with a short description of it for people who cannot see it.
3. Submit it for listing. A new listing is **reviewed before it appears**, so it is not instant, and the owner can see its progress.
4. Once live, the owner can add events, offers and updates from their dashboard, and can build a simple page for their business from the same details.

If the business is already on the site in some form, an owner can **claim** it. Anyone can also use **Suggest a business** on the site; that goes to a private review and does not publish anything.

## 4. Message templates (English; Welsh needs a fluent speaker's check before use)

**Short message: for a public post or comment, or a reply to someone who has asked (not a cold private message to a sole trader or partnership, which needs their consent first)**

> Hi, I'm [name] from OurValleys, a free local guide to businesses and events in the valleys (ourvalleys.co.uk). We'd love [Business] to be listed. It's free, takes about ten minutes, and you stay in control of what's shown. Happy to help you get set up. If you'd rather not, no problem at all.

**Longer message: to say in person, or to send to someone who has agreed to hear from you (or to a limited company's corporate address, with your name and a way to opt out)**

> I'm [name], and I'm building OurValleys, a free guide to the local businesses, events and offers in [place] and the surrounding valleys. Each business has its own page that it controls: opening hours, contact details, photos, offers and events. There are no fees, no adverts and no paid ranking, and nothing appears about a business unless its owner puts it there. A new listing is checked by us before it goes live.
>
> If you'd like [Business] to be one of the first, you can start a listing at ourvalleys.co.uk (choose List your business). I can also sit down with you and do it together. Would you like that?

**For a community group, club or event organiser**

> OurValleys has an Events page for what's happening locally, and it's free to add yours. If [Group] runs regular sessions, meets or one-off events, you can list them with the date, time, place and a contact, and they'll show up for people searching the area. I can help you add the first one.

**A flyer or noticeboard line**

> Run a local business or club? Get listed free on OurValleys, the local guide to the valleys: ourvalleys.co.uk

## 5. After someone agrees

- Help them through the steps in section 3 only if they ask, and never type in a business's details on their behalf without them seeing and agreeing to every field.
- Check that what is published is true: the opening hours, the phone number, the address they have chosen to show.
- Once a few are live, share the page address with them to post on their own channels. A local news mention or a post in the community group helps more than another round of messages.

## 6. What to measure

Count: businesses contacted, replies, accounts created, listings submitted, listings live, and events added. The dashboard's insights show visits to each listing once there are any. Do not record personal details about the people contacted beyond what is needed to follow up with them.
