# 120. Privacy policy: wording, evidence and what the owner must supply

Written 16 November 2026 and completed 17 November 2026. The wording is `src/lib/privacy-policy.ts`, shown at `/policies/privacy`. It replaces the five-paragraph baseline. It is a factual description of what the application does. It is not legal advice. On 17 November 2026 the owner delegated the drafting, checking and approval of this policy to the engineering agent, so `POLICIES_APPROVED` is set on that authority (`docs/119` §3); the owner can withdraw it at any time by setting the variable back to `false`.

`src/lib/privacy-policy.test.ts` ties the figures and names in the text to the constants and files below, so a change to the code that makes the policy untrue fails a test instead of going unnoticed.

## 2. What is settled, and what is still the owner's

Settled on 17 November 2026 (sources in §1): who the controller is, a contact route, the legal reason for each purpose, the overseas-transfer safeguards, children, rights and the date.

Still not in the policy, deliberately, because no source establishes it:

1. **An ICO registration number.** A sole trader who handles personal data usually has to pay the ICO's data protection fee, unless exempt. The policy states no number and makes no claim either way. The owner should confirm the registration (or the exemption) with the ICO; it does not change what the policy says.
2. **The sole trader's own name.** The policy uses "VEXI, a sole trader trading as VEXI", matching the owner's published policy. If a stricter form of the controller's name is wanted, change the first section and `privacy-policy.test.ts` together.
3. **A privacy contact address.** The route is the vexi.co.uk form. If a dedicated address (for example `privacy@`) is set up, replace the sentence in two places: "Who is responsible for your data" and "Your rights and choices".
4. **A Welsh version**, reviewed by a fluent speaker. The page carries a note that policy wording is English only.
5. **Specialist legal review.** The policy has been drafted and checked against the code and sources, not reviewed by a solicitor. The owner chose to rely on that.

## 3. Statements that depend on other decisions

- **Cloudflare analytics.** The policy says Cloudflare "may also give us aggregate figures on visits and performance". Cloudflare Web Analytics is injected at the edge today (`docs/119` §7). If the owner switches it off in the Cloudflare dashboard, delete that sentence. Cloudflare's own pages do not say whether it uses cookies, so the policy makes no claim either way.
- **Uploaded pictures.** The providers section says Railway hosts the pictures and documents businesses upload, and Cloudflare keeps temporary copies. That is true once the Railway Bucket is connected (`docs/119` §1, gap 1): files are stored in a private Railway Bucket and served by the site's `/media` route, and Cloudflare caches them at its edge. If the storage ever moves to R2 or another provider, change that sentence.
- **Postmark open tracking.** The sentence is true while `POSTMARK_TRACK_OPENS` is `true`. The test fails if that changes.

## 4. Maintenance

Add a section, and a test line, whenever the site starts collecting something new: a cookie, a third-party script, a new analytics event, a longer retention period or a new provider.
